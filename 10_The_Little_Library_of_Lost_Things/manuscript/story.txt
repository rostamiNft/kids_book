THE LITTLE LIBRARY OF LOST THINGS
by Hamidreza Rostami

Target audience: Children aged 3-9 years
Moral lesson: Every lost thing has a story, and every story deserves to be heard. Kindness is mostly just listening well.
Visual style: High-quality 3D animated children's illustration (cinematic children's animation), warm cosy painterly rendering, honey-gold lamplight, dust motes in sunbeams, rich wood and wool textures.
Format: Portrait orientation, 9:16 aspect ratio, 768x1376 px, RGB, one image per page, 20 pages total (1 front cover + 18 story pages + 1 back cover).

CHARACTER LOCK (repeat in every prompt):
Ms. Willow - a kind elderly woman in her seventies, small and round, with soft silver hair in a neat bun, round brass-rimmed glasses, gentle crinkly eyes, rosy cheeks and a warm smile. She wears a deep teal knitted cardigan over a cream blouse, a long burgundy skirt and a small silver locket. She must look exactly the same on every page.

CHILD CHARACTERS:
- Sam: a little boy about six, warm brown skin, short curly black hair, big dark eyes, a mustard-yellow jumper and blue jeans. He appears on pages 5 to 14.
- Emily: a little girl about seven, pale skin, long straight auburn hair in two plaits, freckles, a soft sage-green dress. She appears on pages 16 to 19.
- Mia: a little girl about five, east-asian, straight black hair in a bob with a red hair clip, green dungarees.
- Jake: a little boy about six, blond hair, round face, a striped blue-and-white t-shirt.

SETTING: The Little Library of Lost Things, at the end of Maple Street between a bakery and a flower shop. A small crooked two-storey building of warm honey-coloured stone with a bright RED door, a bay window and a chimney that always curls smoke. Inside: towering mismatched bookshelves, a worn green armchair by a fireplace, a wooden counter, a brass bell, jars of buttons and a low shelf of labelled lost things.

TEXT RULE: all text is painted into the illustration. Narrative prose carries no quotation marks. Spoken lines keep double quotation marks exactly. Avoid contractions that invite stray apostrophes (write "do not", "did not", "it is", "cannot").

Title (embedded in artwork): THE LITTLE LIBRARY OF LOST THINGS

== PAGE 2 ==
Text: At the end of Maple Street, between the bakery and the flower shop, stood a little library. It was not like other libraries. This one was small and cozy, with a red door and a chimney that always had smoke curling from it. And it had a special sign.
Art: A charming street scene at the end of Maple Street in warm afternoon light. On the left a bakery with a striped awning and bread in its window; on the right a flower shop spilling buckets of bright blossoms onto the pavement. Between them, the little library: small, crooked, honey-stone, with a bright red door, a bay window and a curl of chimney smoke. A hand-painted wooden sign hangs above the door reading "The Little Library of Lost Things". Children's bicycles lean nearby, leaves drifting.

== PAGE 3 ==
Text: Inside, Ms. Willow kept the library. She had kind eyes, a warm smile, and a special gift.
Art: Inside the library: a tall warm room of mismatched bookshelves reaching the ceiling, a worn green armchair, a fireplace, a wooden counter with a brass bell. Ms. Willow stands at the counter, silver bun, round glasses, deep teal cardigan, warm smile, one hand resting on an open book. Golden lamplight, floating dust motes, stacks of books everywhere.

== PAGE 4 ==
Text: She could hear the stories of lost things. Not the stories in books. But the stories of the things themselves.
Art: A magical close-up. Ms. Willow sits in her armchair holding a small brown teddy bear gently up near her ear, her eyes closed, a listening smile on her face. Around the bear, faint soft golden threads of light and tiny drifting images curl upward like memories becoming visible: a garden, a swing, a child's hand. Warm firelight, quiet and tender.

== PAGE 5 ==
Text: One day, a little boy named Sam came into the library. He looked sad and worried.
Art: Sam, a little boy about six with warm brown skin and short curly black hair, stands just inside the red door with his shoulders slumped and his eyes downcast, wearing a mustard-yellow jumper and blue jeans. Behind the counter Ms. Willow looks up with concern. Warm light from the shelves, the brass bell still swinging above the door.

== PAGE 6 ==
Text: "I lost my favorite toy car," he said. "It was red and fast, and I loved it so much." Ms. Willow smiled. "Let us see if we can find it a story to tell."
Art: Sam stands close to the counter holding up a crumpled crayon drawing of a red toy car, his face sad and hopeful. Ms. Willow leans toward him with a gentle kind smile, one hand reaching out. Shelves tower warmly behind them, a small lamp glowing on the counter. Tender and warm.

== PAGE 7 ==
Text: Ms. Willow led Sam to a shelf filled with lost toys. There were dolls, balls, stuffed animals, and yes, cars.
Art: A wonderful wide shot of a long low shelf crammed with lost things, each with a tiny hand-written paper label: dolls with one eye, bright rubber balls, stuffed animals of every shape, wooden blocks, jacks, and a row of small toy cars at the end. Ms. Willow leads Sam along by the hand, both looking at the shelves. Warm lamplight, dust motes.

== PAGE 8 ==
Text: She picked up a small, red toy car. "Is this yours?" Sam's eyes lit up. "Yes! That is my car!"
Art: A close joyful shot. Ms. Willow holds out a small red toy car in both hands at chest height, and Sam's face has erupted into delight, eyes wide and shining, mouth open in a grin, both hands rising to reach for it. The shelf of lost toys is soft behind them. Warm golden light catching the little red car.

== PAGE 9 ==
Text: But then Sam hesitated. "But how did it get here?" Ms. Willow placed the car in Sam's hand. "Every lost thing has a story. Would you like to hear yours?" Sam nodded.
Art: Sam holds the little red car in both hands but his smile has faded into a puzzled frown, his head tilted. Ms. Willow's weathered hands are closing Sam's small hands around the car, hers cupped over his, and she looks down at him kindly. Warm lamplight on their hands. Close and intimate.

== PAGE 10 ==
Text: Ms. Willow closed her eyes and listened. Then she began to speak.
Art: A hushed magical portrait. Ms. Willow sits in her armchair with her eyes closed and her head tilted, listening, one hand open on her knee. The room has gone soft and golden around her, faint glowing threads and tiny sparkles of remembered light curling in the air. The little red car sits on the arm of the chair, Sam's shape a warm blur at the edge. Firelight, deep warmth.

== PAGE 11 ==
Text: "Your car did not want to be lost. It tried to follow you home. It rolled down the sidewalk, turned the corner, even tried to climb a hill." Sam giggled. "A car cannot climb a hill!" Ms. Willow smiled. "This one tried. For you."
Art: A warm storytelling scene. In the glowing upper part of the image, a luminous memory: the little red toy car rolling bravely up a steep green hill with its tiny wheels spinning. In the foreground Sam sits cross-legged on the rug laughing, the real red car in his lap, and Ms. Willow smiles from her armchair. Golden light, cosy and funny.

== PAGE 12 ==
Text: "It rolled all the way to the park," Ms. Willow continued. "And there, it met a little girl who had lost her doll. The car told the doll about you, and the doll told the car to keep going."
Art: A dreamy storytelling image. In the foreground Ms. Willow speaks with her eyes half closed and Sam listens with his mouth open. In the glowing upper half a soft memory scene: the little red car on a park path beside a small lost doll with tangled hair, the two of them facing each other under a great tree, the park golden and quiet. Warm and magical.

== PAGE 13 ==
Text: Sam's eyes were wide. "My car talked to a doll?" Ms. Willow nodded. "Lost things help each other. They know what it is like to be away from home."
Art: A close tender two-shot. Sam's face is full of wonder, eyes round, the red car cradled to his chest. Ms. Willow leans in with a gentle nod and a soft smile, firelight warm on both faces. Deep golden background of books and shadow. Intimate and full of heart.

== PAGE 14 ==
Text: "Finally," Ms. Willow said, "your car rolled to the library. It knew this was a place where lost things could be found." Sam hugged his car. "I did not find it," Ms. Willow said. "It found its way here, because it wanted to come home to you."
Art: Outside the library at dusk, seen low to the ground: the red door glowing, light spilling across the pavement, and a tiny red toy car sitting right in front of the step. In the foreground corner of the same image, Sam hugs the little red car tightly against his cheek with his eyes squeezed shut in happiness while Ms. Willow rests one hand on his curly head. Warm windows, purple evening sky, a curl of chimney smoke.

== PAGE 15 ==
Text: Word spread about the Little Library of Lost Things. More children came, looking for their lost treasures. A girl named Mia found her missing sock. A boy named Jake found his key.
Art: A busy happy library scene. On the left Mia, a little girl about five with a straight black bob in green dungarees and a red hair clip, holds up a long striped sock triumphantly. On the right Jake, a little blond boy in a striped blue-and-white t-shirt, holds a small brass key above his head. Ms. Willow stands between them with her hands clasped, delighted. Warm and lively.

== PAGE 16 ==
Text: One day, a little girl named Emily came in. She looked very sad. "I lost my best friend," she said. "Not a toy. A real friend. She moved away."
Art: A quiet gentle image. Emily, a little girl about seven with long straight auburn hair in two plaits and freckles, in a soft sage-green dress, stands alone just inside the red door, her hands clasped, her head down, tears in her eyes. Ms. Willow has come around the counter and stopped short, her face full of concern, one hand at her silver locket. The warm library seems to hush around them.

== PAGE 17 ==
Text: Ms. Willow thought for a moment. Then she took Emily to a special shelf. On it was a small wooden box, old, with a beautiful carving on the top. "This is the Friendship Box. It helps friends stay connected, even when they are far apart."
Art: Ms. Willow and Emily stand before a tall narrow shelf where a single small wooden box rests on a velvet cloth, glowing softly. The lid is beautifully carved with two birds facing each other. Ms. Willow gestures toward it with an open hand; Emily looks up at it, her tears drying, a flicker of hope on her face. Warm golden lamplight.

== PAGE 18 ==
Text: Ms. Willow opened the box. Inside were two small, matching stones. "Take one stone," Ms. Willow said. "And give the other to your friend. As long as you both have a stone, you will always be connected."
Art: A beautiful close-up. The open carved wooden box in Ms. Willow's hands holds two small smooth matching stones, pale rose-gold, glowing softly. Emily's small hand reaches in and lifts one stone; Ms. Willow smiles down at her. Warm firelight, dust motes, the shelf of books soft behind. Tender and full of promise.

== PAGE 19 ==
Text: Emily wrote a letter to her friend. She told her about the library, about the Friendship Box, and about how much she missed her. Ms. Willow mailed the letter, with the second stone inside. And every time Emily held her stone, she felt a little closer to her friend.
Art: Emily sits at a small wooden desk writing a letter, the smooth stone beside the paper, her face calm and softly happy. Beside her stands an envelope with the second stone resting on it. Through the open red door in the background Ms. Willow is seen posting the letter into a red postbox on the sunlit street. Warm amber light, the library cosy around her. Hopeful and warm.

== PAGE 20 (BACK COVER) ==
Blurb (embedded in artwork, three clearly separated paragraphs):
At the end of Maple Street there is a small library with a red door, kept by Ms. Willow, who can hear the stories of lost things.
When Sam loses his favourite red toy car, Ms. Willow has a story to tell him. And when Emily loses something no shelf can hold, she finds something better.
A warm story about kindness, listening, and discovering that every lost thing has a story, and every story deserves to be heard.
