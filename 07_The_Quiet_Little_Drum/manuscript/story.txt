THE QUIET LITTLE DRUM
by Hamidreza Rostami

Target audience: Children aged 3-9 years
Moral lesson: You do not have to be loud to matter. Sometimes the most important sounds are the ones you have to listen very carefully to hear.
Visual style: High-quality 3D animated children's illustration (cinematic children's animation), warm painterly rendering with golden dust motes in sunbeams, glossy toy-like surfaces, cozy and inviting.
Format: Portrait orientation, 9:16 aspect ratio, 768x1376 px, RGB, one image per page, 20 pages total.

CHARACTER LOCK (repeat in every prompt):
Drum - a small round drum character, about the size of a cookie tin, with a smooth honey-brown polished wooden body, a soft cream leather top stretched tight, small brass tacks and tiny tuning pegs around the rim, two gentle dark expressive eyes and a small mouth painted onto the wooden body, and two tiny wooden feet. He is alive and friendly. He must look like a warm wooden drum with a face, NOT a toy robot, and NOT a cartoon face floating on a cylinder.

CHARACTER LOCK:
Lily - a little girl about six years old with soft wavy brown hair to her shoulders, big curious hazel eyes, light freckles, a simple pale-yellow dress with a cream collar and white socks. She appears in the second half of the book and must look identical on every page.

SUPPORTING CHARACTERS:
- Mr. Harmony: an elderly shopkeeper with round brass glasses, a fluffy grey moustache, a burgundy apron over a cream shirt, kind crinkly eyes.
- The kitten: a tiny grey tabby kitten with white paws, a white chest and big green eyes.
- Violin: an elegant amber violin with a face, warm and curious.
- Trumpet: a shiny brass trumpet with a face, brash, loud and friendly.
- Piano: a warm brown upright piano with a face, gentle and worried.

SETTING: Mr. Harmony's music shop - a warm dusty shop full of wooden shelves, brass instruments, stacked sheet music and golden afternoon sunbeams; and later Lily's cozy bedroom at night with a big window and a patchwork quilt.

TEXT RULE: all text is painted into the illustration. Narrative prose carries no quotation marks; spoken lines keep their quotation marks exactly. Avoid contractions that invite stray apostrophes (write "cannot", "did not", "does not").

Text: The trumpet blasted. The violin sang. The piano tinkled. The cymbals crashed. And then there was Drum.

Text: Drum was small and round, with a smooth wooden body and a soft leather top. He loved music. He loved rhythm. He loved the way the other instruments made the air dance.

== PAGE 5 ==
Text: But Drum had a secret. He could not make a sound.
Art: A quiet, tender close-up. Drum sits alone, his eyes downcast and sad, his smooth cream leather top untouched and unmarked. Far behind him the other instruments glow and play, blurred and warm, their sound a soft haze. A single shaft of light falls on Drum. Melancholy but gentle.

== PAGE 6 ==
Text: "Why do you not play with us?" asked Violin, her strings humming.
Art: Elegant amber Violin leans down toward small Drum, her bow lowered, her face kind and curious, her strings faintly shimmering. Drum looks up at her, uncertain, his little wooden feet together. Warm wooden shop background. Friendly and gentle.

== PAGE 7 ==
Text: "Of course you can!" said Trumpet, blasting a loud note. "Just hit your top!" Drum tried. He tried hard. But no sound came out.
Art: A funny, tender scene. Trumpet leans in close, cheeks puffed, a bold burst of bright notes flying from his bell. Drum stands beside him having just given his top a hopeful little whack with one wooden edge, his eyes squeezed shut in effort, his cream top perfectly silent. Violin watches with a worried tilt of her head. Warm light.

== PAGE 8 ==
Text: "Maybe you are broken," said Piano, her keys clicking softly. Drum's heart sank. "Maybe I am."

Text: One day, a little girl named Lily came into the shop. She had soft brown hair and curious eyes.

Text: She walked around, looking at all the instruments. She listened to the trumpet's blast. She admired the violin's shine. She tapped the piano's keys. Then she saw Drum.

== PAGE 15 ==
Text: "You are going to be my special drum," she said. "Even if you cannot make noise, you can still be with me." Drum felt warm inside. No one had ever talked to him like that before.

== PAGE 16 ==
Text: One night, as Lily slept, Drum heard a sound. A soft, sad sound. It was coming from outside. A little kitten was mewing, lost and scared.

Outside the moonlit window, a tiny grey tabby kitten with white paws and big green eyes sits on a branch, alone and frightened, mewing. Cool blue moonlight outside, warm dark room inside.

== PAGE 17 ==
Text: Drum wanted to help. But how? He could not make noise to call for help. Then Drum had an idea. He rolled to the window and began to tap. Not on his top. On the glass. Tap. Tap. Tap.
Art: Dramatic, delightful night moment. Drum presses his wooden rim and one tiny foot against the window glass, tapping it, with little concentric ripple rings and tiny bright tap-marks glowing where he touches. Outside, the little grey kitten looks up, ears forward, hopeful. Moonlight blue and a warm rim of light on Drum.

== PAGE 18 ==
Text: Lily woke up. She heard the soft tapping and saw Drum at the window. She looked outside and saw the kitten. She opened the window and brought the kitten inside.
Art: Lily kneels at the open window in her nightgown with the moonlight behind her, cradling the little grey tabby kitten in her arms, its white paws tucked in, while Drum sits beside them on the sill, glowing with quiet pride. Night blue outside, warm lamp glow inside.

== PAGE 19 ==
Text: Lily hugged Drum. "Thank you, Drum," she said. "You helped me find the kitten!" Then she held Drum close to her ear and whispered, "Maybe you are meant to be quiet." And then Drum heard it. The softest sound, like a heartbeat. It was his sound.
Art: The emotional payoff, a single intimate scene. Lily sits on her bed in soft lamplight, holding Drum up to her ear with both hands, her eyes closed in concentration and a small wondering smile. The kitten is curled asleep on the quilt beside her. Around Drum, the faintest soft golden ripples and tiny glowing music notes drift out, delicate and quiet, showing his gentle new sound. Warm, magical, still.

== PAGE 20 (BACK COVER) ==
Blurb (embedded in artwork):
Drum lives in Mr. Harmony's music shop, where every instrument has a voice. Every instrument except Drum.
When a lost kitten calls in the night, the littlest drum in the shop finds a way to help without making a single loud sound.
A gentle story about kindness, patience, and discovering that the most important sounds are the ones you have to listen very carefully to hear.
