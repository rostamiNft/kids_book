THE TINY GIANT
by Hamidreza Rostami

Target audience: Children aged 3-9 years
Moral lesson: Being big is not about size. It is about the heart, and what you do with it.
Visual style: High-quality 3D animated children's illustration (cinematic children's animation), lush miniature forest world seen from a tiny scale, warm dappled sunlight through leaves, glowing bokeh, glossy toy-like rendering.
Format: Portrait orientation, 9:16 aspect ratio, 768x1376 px, RGB, one image per page, 20 pages total.

SCALE RULE (critical for this book):
Everything in the forest is drawn at ENORMOUS scale relative to Pip. A blade of grass is taller than he is. A pebble is a hill. A mushroom is a chair or a stage. A raindrop is a bath. Keep this tiny-creature perspective on every page.

CHARACTER LOCK (repeat in every prompt):
Pip - a very small round creature with soft mint-green fur, a cream belly, huge round ears like a fennec fox, big bright amber eyes, a small black nose, tiny paws and feet, a tuft of fluff on top of his head. Cheerful, proud, determined, and absolutely convinced he is a giant.

Supporting characters:
- Squirrel(s): plump cheerful red-brown squirrels with big fluffy tails and bright clever eyes. One older, wiser squirrel speaks the closing line.
- Rabbit: a soft brown rabbit with long ears, a gentle face and big hind feet.
- Fox: a young orange fox cub with a white chest, big ears and hopeful eyes.
- Mice family: three tiny grey mice with pink ears.
- Birds: two small round blue-and-grey songbirds.

TEXT RULE: all text is painted into the illustration. Narrative prose carries no quotation marks; spoken lines keep their quotation marks exactly. Avoid contractions that invite stray apostrophes (write "did not", "cannot").

== PAGE 2 ==
Text: Pip was small. Very small. So small that he could hide behind a single blade of grass. So small that a raindrop could give him a bath.
Art: Dazzling miniature forest. Towering grass like skyscrapers, giant flowers, an enormous acorn on the moss. Pip tiny at the bottom center, hands on hips, looking up.

== PAGE 3 ==
Text: So small that the other forest creatures often forgot he was there. But Pip did not mind being small. In fact, Pip thought he was a giant.
Art: Close character portrait. Pip on a small flat stone, chest puffed, chin high, one paw in a proud salute. Giant sparkling dewdrops as big as his head around him.

== PAGE 4 ==
Text: "I am Pip the Giant!" he would declare, standing on top of a pebble.
Art: Low-angle hero shot. Pip on top of a smooth round grey pebble, one paw thrown up in victory, mouth open mid-declaration.

== PAGE 5 ==
Text: The squirrels would giggle. "You're not a giant, Pip. You're the smallest creature in the forest!" The rabbits would laugh. "A giant? You're smaller than my foot!"
Art: Pip on his pebble, arms crossed, unbothered. Two squirrels giggle, a brown rabbit laughs with one long hind foot stretched out beside Pip.

== PAGE 6 ==
Text: But Pip just smiled. "You'll see," he would say. "One day, I'll do something so big, so giant, that everyone will know I'm a giant too!"
Art: Warm close-up. Pip points up and forward, eyes full of determination. The enormous glowing forest behind him, squirrel and rabbit soft behind.

== PAGE 7 ==
Text: One day, a great wind blew through the forest. It was strong and wild, and it knocked down many things.
Art: Dramatic windy scene. Leaves, petals and branches fly in curved motion, giant grass bends flat, cool grey-green light. Pip crouches low gripping a mossy root.

== PAGE 8 ==
Text: A bird's nest fell from a tree. A family of mice lost their home. A little fox's favorite play area was covered in branches. The forest creatures were sad and worried.
Art: Aftermath in one scene: fallen woven nest with two worried birds, three mice looking at their crushed grass home, fox cub downcast beside fallen branches. Pip appears exactly once, watching.

== PAGE 9 ==
Text: Pip saw the trouble. He knew he had to help. But he was so small. What could he do? Then he had an idea. A giant idea.
Art: Eureka close-up. Pip's ears snap up, eyes wide, one paw raised, a golden star sparkle above his head.

== PAGE 10 ==
Text: He scurried to the center of the forest, where the biggest tree stood. At the base of the tree was a hole, just the right size for Pip.
Art: Epic scale. Enormous mossy tree trunk like a canyon wall, small dark hole between the roots. Pip runs toward it at full speed.

== PAGE 11 ==
Text: Pip squeezed into the hole and began to dig. He dug and dug, making the hole bigger and deeper. "What are you doing, Pip?" asked a squirrel, peeking in. "I'm making a new home!" Pip said.
Art: Inside the hollow. Pip digs with both paws, soil flying in an arc. The red-brown squirrel peeks over the rim.

== PAGE 12 ==
Text: The other creatures watched as Pip worked. He was small, but he was fast. And he did not stop.
Art: The squirrel, the rabbit, two birds and the mice gather at the tree root, watching Pip half inside the hole, tail poking out. Golden light returns.

== PAGE 13 ==
Text: Soon, the hole was big enough for the mice family. They moved in, safe and warm. But Pip was not done.
Art: Cozy hollow home with three happy mice on moss bedding, warm glow inside. Pip stands outside, paws on hips, beaming.

== PAGE 14 ==
Text: He climbed the tree. For Pip, it was like climbing a mountain. High above, he found the bird's nest, tangled in branches.
Art: Epic vertical shot up the trunk. Pip clings to a bark ledge, looking up toward the distant tangled nest. God-rays through the canopy.

== PAGE 15 ==
Text: Pip carefully climbed to the nest. He used his small, nimble paws to untangle it, then carried it piece by piece to a safer branch.
Art: High in the canopy, Pip stands on a tangle of woven twigs, easing two branches apart, tongue out in concentration. Two blue-grey birds watch from a nearby branch.

== PAGE 16 ==
Text: The birds chirped happily as they settled into their repaired nest. Pip climbed down and saw the fox, still looking sad near the pile of branches.
Art: Two moods. Above, the repaired nest on a branch with two happy chirping birds. Below, Pip at the trunk looks at the sad fox cub beside a heap of branches.

== PAGE 17 ==
Text: "What is wrong?" Pip asked. "My favorite play area is all covered," the fox said. "I cannot play hide and seek anymore."
Art: Tender eye-level talk. Pip on a mossy rock, one paw on the fox's big front paw. The fox sits with drooping ears and hopeful eyes.

== PAGE 18 ==
Text: Pip looked at the branches. They were big and heavy. Much bigger than him. But Pip had an idea.
Art: Pip stands before an enormous jumbled mountain of fallen branches, arms crossed, paw at chin, a golden sparkle of inspiration near his head.

== PAGE 19 ==
Text: He gathered all the small creatures, and together they moved the branches. It took a long time, but at last the play area was clear. "You may be small," said the oldest squirrel, "but you have the heart of a giant."
Art: Layered finale. Mid-frame: the mice, squirrels, rabbit and beetles haul a thick fallen branch together with grass ropes, Pip leading at the front. Below: the cleared play area, the fox cub leaping with joy, and the wise old squirrel leaning down to speak to tiny Pip.

Blurb (embedded in artwork):
Pip is the smallest creature in the whole forest. So of course he tells everyone he is a giant.
When a great wind knocks down nests and homes, tiny Pip sets out to do something truly enormous.
A warm story about kindness, courage, and discovering that being big has nothing to do with size.
