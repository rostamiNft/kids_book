PAGE 5 (Story begins):
Pip was small.

Very small.

So small that he could hide behind a single blade of grass. So small that a raindrop could give him a bath. So small that the other forest creatures often forgot he was there.

But Pip didn't mind being small.

In fact, Pip thought he was a giant.

[Illustration: Pip, a tiny round creature with oversized ears and a cheerful expression, standing in a forest where everything is huge compared to him - towering flowers, massive blades of grass, a giant acorn. He looks proud and confident.]

===
PAGE 6-7:
"I am Pip the Giant!" he would declare, standing on top of a pebble.

The squirrels would giggle. "You're not a giant, Pip. You're the smallest creature in the forest!"

The rabbits would laugh. "A giant? You're smaller than my foot!"

But Pip just smiled. "You'll see," he would say. "One day, I'll do something so big, so giant, that everyone will know I'm a giant too!"

[Illustration: Pip standing on a pebble, declaring himself a giant. Squirrels and rabbits laugh around him. Pip looks determined.]

===
PAGE 8-9:
One day, a great wind blew through the forest. It was strong and wild, and it knocked down many things.

A bird's nest fell from a tree. A family of mice lost their home. A little fox's favorite play area was covered in branches.

The forest creatures were sad and worried.

[Illustration: A windy forest scene. A bird's nest is on the ground. Mice look at their damaged home. A fox looks at a pile of branches. Pip watches from a safe spot.]

===
PAGE 10-11:
Pip saw the trouble. He knew he had to help.

But he was so small. What could he do?

Then he had an idea. A giant idea.

He scurried to the center of the forest, where the biggest tree stood. At the base of the tree was a hole—just the right size for Pip.

[Illustration: Pip running toward a large tree with a hole at its base. The forest is still windy, with leaves and small branches blowing around.]

===
PAGE 12-13:
Pip squeezed into the hole and began to dig. He dug and dug, making the hole bigger and deeper.

"What are you doing, Pip?" asked a squirrel, peeking in.

"I'm making a new home!" Pip said, his small paws working quickly.

[Illustration: Pip inside the tree hole, digging. A squirrel peeks in, looking curious. Dirt is flying out behind Pip.]

===
PAGE 14-15:
The other creatures watched as Pip worked. He was small, but he was fast. And he didn't stop.

Soon, the hole was big enough for the mice family. They moved in, safe and warm.

But Pip wasn't done.

[Illustration: The mice family moving into the enlarged hole in the tree. They look happy and safe. Pip is still digging, making the hole even bigger.]

===
PAGE 16-17:
He climbed the tree (which, for Pip, was like climbing a mountain) and found the bird's nest.

It was tangled in branches, high above the ground.

Pip carefully climbed to the nest. He used his small, nimble paws to untangle it. Then, he carried it piece by piece to a safer branch.

[Illustration: Pip high in the tree, working on the tangled bird's nest. He's using his paws to carefully untangle the branches. Birds watch from a nearby branch.]

===
PAGE 18-19:
The birds chirped happily as they settled into their repaired nest.

Pip climbed down and saw the fox, still looking sad near the pile of branches.

"What's wrong?" Pip asked.

"My favorite play area is all covered," the fox said. "I can't play hide and seek anymore."

[Illustration: Pip talking to the fox. The fox looks sad, sitting next to a pile of branches. Pip looks thoughtful.]

===
PAGE 20-21:
Pip looked at the branches. They were big and heavy. Much bigger than him.

But Pip had an idea.

He gathered all the small creatures—the mice, the squirrels, even a few bugs—and they worked together to move the branches.

[Illustration: Pip organizing the small creatures. Mice, squirrels, and bugs are all working together to move branches. Pip is directing them.]

===
PAGE 22-23:
It took a long time. And it was hard work. But finally, the play area was clear.

The fox's eyes lit up. "Thank you, Pip! Now I can play again!"

Pip smiled. "You're welcome. That's what giants do."

[Illustration: The play area is clear. The fox looks happy and is starting to play. Pip stands nearby, looking proud.]

===
PAGE 24-25:
Word of Pip's good deeds spread through the forest.

The birds told their friends. The mice told their family. The fox told everyone he met.

Soon, all the forest creatures knew about the tiny creature who had done such big, giant things.

[Illustration: Forest creatures talking to each other, spreading the news about Pip. Pip walks by, looking happy.]

===
PAGE 26-27:
The next day, as Pip was resting on a mushroom (which, for him, was like a big, comfy chair), the forest creatures gathered around.

"Pip," said the oldest squirrel, "we have something to say."

Pip sat up, curious.

[Illustration: Pip sitting on a large mushroom. Forest creatures are gathered around him, looking serious but kind.]

===
PAGE 28 (Final page):
"You may be small," said the squirrel. "But you have the heart of a giant.

And that makes you the biggest creature in the forest."

Pip beamed. He had known it all along.

He was Pip the Giant.

[Illustration: Pip standing tall on his mushroom, looking proud. The forest creatures are around him, smiling. The forest is peaceful and beautiful.]
