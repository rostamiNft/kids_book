THE LAST LEAF OF AUTUMN
by Hamidreza Rostami

Target audience: Children aged 3-9 years
Moral lesson: Letting go is not the end of something. It is the beginning of something new.
Visual style: High-quality 3D animated children's illustration (cinematic children's animation), warm golden autumn light, painterly rendering with glowing sunbeams, soft bokeh, rich russet and amber palette.
Format: Portrait orientation, 9:16 aspect ratio, 768x1376 px, RGB, one image per page, 20 pages total.

CHARACTER LOCK (repeat in every prompt):
Rustle - a bright maple leaf with a soft friendly face painted into his surface, two gentle dark eyes, a small kind mouth, and rosy warm cheeks. He has a slim russet-brown stem, gently lobed maple points and a slightly curled edge. His colour changes through the book, exactly as listed on each page, and he is always the SAME leaf shape and face.

SUPPORTING CHARACTERS:
- Amber: a warm golden-orange maple leaf with a cheerful face and a long stem.
- Golden: a bright yellow maple leaf with a laughing face.
- Crimson: a deep red maple leaf with a bold friendly face.
- The blue jay: a vivid blue jay with a white chest, a black crest and bright curious eyes.
- Emma: a little girl about six years old with wavy chestnut hair, a mustard-yellow coat, a rust-red scarf, dark green boots.
- The wind: never drawn as a body or a creature. Show it only as swirling ribbons of air, drifting leaves and soft spiral lines of light.

SETTING: a great maple tree in a park across the autumn, then the fallen-leaf carpet on the ground, then a moonlit night, then dawn.

TEXT RULE: all text is painted into the illustration. Narrative prose carries no quotation marks; spoken lines keep double quotation marks exactly. Avoid contractions that invite stray apostrophes (write "I am", "do not", "it is").

== PAGE 1 (FRONT COVER) ==
Title (embedded in artwork): THE LAST LEAF OF AUTUMN
Credit (embedded in artwork): Created by Hamidreza Rostami
Art: Rustle, a vivid red-and-gold maple leaf with a gentle face, clings to his branch high in a great maple tree, seen close and beautiful, glowing with backlit golden autumn sun. Behind and below him the tree is nearly bare, and the ground is a soft carpet of fallen gold and red leaves. Warm russet and amber palette, glowing bokeh.

== PAGE 2 ==
Text: Rustle loved his branch. It was the perfect branch, high up in the great maple tree. Just the right spot for catching the morning sun. Just the right place for feeling the cool breeze. And it was the perfect branch for watching the world.
Art: One establishing image holding both the great tree and the view. We look up along a great branch; Rustle hangs on it glowing in warm morning sun. Behind and below him the park opens out in soft focus: winding paths, a bench, a small pond, tiny distant figures, morning mist. Warm sunbeams, glowing bokeh.
NOTE: pages 2 and 3 of the original outline were merged into this single page so that the ending (the fall and Emma) could get its full pages without losing the moral.

== PAGE 3 ==
Text: Rustle had spent all spring growing. All summer dancing in the wind. And now, autumn had come.
Art: A seasonal story in one frame: the same tree with three moods layered softly, spring green buds at the top, full summer green in the middle, golden autumn at the bottom, the light warm and mellow. Rustle hangs at the centre, glowing, a few leaves drifting past him.

== PAGE 4 ==
Text: The other leaves were changing. Turning gold. Turning red. Turning orange. And one by one, they were letting go.
Art: A rich autumn canopy. Around Rustle, leaves turn gold, red and orange and several fall, caught mid-drift on beams of golden light. Rustle is still green among them, clinging on, watching. Warm luminous bokeh.

== PAGE 5 ==
Text: "Time to go, Rustle!" called his friend Amber, as she twirled down to the ground. "The ground is soft!" said Golden, landing beside her. "And the wind is fun!"
Art: Amber (golden-orange) and Golden (bright yellow), both with happy faces, twirling down through warm air in a graceful spiral. Amber calls back up toward Rustle. Golden lands on the leaf carpet with a happy bounce.

== PAGE 6 ==
Text: But Rustle shook his stem. "I am not ready. Not yet."
Art: Close tender shot. Rustle on his branch, still green, gripping the twig firmly, his face set and determined, eyebrows drawn. Behind him the tree is half bare and gold light pours through the gaps.

== PAGE 7 ==
Text: The days grew cooler. The winds grew stronger. More leaves fell. The tree was getting bare.
Art: Colder, grander scene. The great tree mostly bare, dark branches crossing a pale sky. Long sweeping ribbons of wind. Leaves tumble away. Cool blue-grey light with a low warm sun behind.

== PAGE 8 ==
Text: "You will be the last one!" called Crimson, as she fluttered down. "That is okay," Rustle said. "I like it here."
Art: Crimson, a deep red maple leaf with a bold friendly face, waves up at Rustle as she flutters down. Rustle clings above, mostly green with the first gold, smiling bravely.

== PAGE 9 ==
Text: Then, one morning, Rustle noticed something. He was changing too. His edges were turning. From green to gold. From gold to red.
Art: Close-up of Rustle looking down at himself in surprise. His surface mid-transformation: green centre, gold spreading through the veins, deep red along the edges and points. Crisp morning light, dew drops.

== PAGE 10 ==
Text: He was becoming beautiful.
Art: Celebration close-up. Rustle at full autumn glory, deep red, gold and orange glowing like stained glass, backlit by low golden sun, every vein lit, his face full of wondering joy.

== PAGE 11 ==
Text: "You are the most beautiful leaf on the tree!" said a blue jay. Rustle blushed, if a leaf can blush. "Do you really think so?" "Of course!" said the blue jay. "Look at you! All those colours!"
Art: A vivid blue jay perched right beside Rustle on the same twig, leaning in and chatting cheerfully, one wing lifted. Rustle glows red and gold with bashful rosy cheeks, puffing up proudly. Soft blue sky, low warm sun flaring between bare branches.
NOTE: pages 12 and 13 of the original outline were merged into this single page.

== PAGE 12 ==
Text: But then the blue jay flew away, and Rustle was alone again.
Art: Quiet, wistful. The blue jay is a small blue shape flying away into the distance. Rustle hangs alone on the great bare branch, small against the wide cool sky, his colours bright but his face lonely. Wide empty space, soft grey-gold light.

== PAGE 13 ==
Text: The wind blew. Stronger this time. Rustle held on tight. "I am not ready," he whispered.
Art: Dramatic wind scene. Long swirling ribbons of wind tear past the bare branches, leaves streaking sideways. Rustle clings desperately, stem stretched and strained, his face frightened but determined. Cool dramatic light.

== PAGE 14 ==
Text: That night, Rustle heard a soft sound. It was the wind, singing through the branches.
Art: Night. A huge full moon behind bare black branches, silver light everywhere. The wind is only soft luminous silver spiral ribbons curling through the branches, almost like music. Rustle hangs on his twig, tiny and pale, listening.

== PAGE 15 ==
Text: "Let go, little one," the wind whispered. "It is time." "But I do not want to fall," Rustle said. "Falling is not the end," the wind said. "It is the beginning of something new."
Art: Same moonlit night, closer on Rustle. Soft silver wind ribbons wrap gently around him like a kind hand, lifting one of his points. His face is thoughtful, sad and hopeful at once, his stem still gripping. Red and gold catch a faint silver glow.

== PAGE 16 ==
Text: Rustle thought about what the wind had said. He looked down at the ground, covered in a soft colourful carpet of leaves. He looked up at the sky, clear and full of stars. And he looked at himself. He was the most beautiful he had ever been.
Art: A three-level view in one image: below, the ground far down covered in a glowing carpet of gold, red and orange leaves; above, a deep clear sky full of stars; in the middle Rustle himself, glowing deep red and gold, gazing around with wonder.

== PAGE 17 ==
Text: The next morning, the sun rose. The wind blew gently. And Rustle made a decision. He took a deep breath, if a leaf can breathe. He loosened his grip. And he let go.
Art: Dawn. Warm pink and gold sunrise floods the bare tree. Rustle's stem is just releasing from the twig, a tiny gap opening, his leaf lifting slightly. His face is calm, brave and peaceful.

== PAGE 18 ==
Text: The wind carried Rustle down, gently, like a lullaby. He twirled. He danced. Falling was not scary. It was wonderful. He landed softly, and the leaves welcomed him.
Art: Joyful fall and welcome in one frame. Upper half: Rustle tumbling and dancing through golden morning air, laughing, sun catching his edges. Lower half: the deep leaf carpet glowing gold, red and orange, two leaves with friendly faces turned up to welcome him.

== PAGE 19 ==
Text: That afternoon, a little girl named Emma came walking through the park. She saw the pile of leaves and smiled. She picked up Rustle and held him high. "You are the most beautiful of all!" she said. And Rustle knew. He had not disappeared. He had just begun a new adventure.
Art: Warm glowing finale. Emma, about six, chestnut wavy hair, mustard-yellow coat, rust-red scarf, dark green boots, stands in a leaf-carpeted park holding Rustle high in both hands, laughing with delight. Rustle glows red and gold, proud and peaceful.

== PAGE 20 (BACK COVER) ==
Blurb (embedded in artwork):
Rustle the maple leaf loves his branch. It is the perfect branch, high in the great maple tree, with the best view in the whole park.
When autumn comes and every other leaf lets go, Rustle holds on. He is not ready. Not yet.
A warm story about courage, change, and discovering that letting go can be the beginning of something beautiful.
