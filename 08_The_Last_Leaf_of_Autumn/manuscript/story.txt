THE LAST LEAF OF AUTUMN
by Hamidreza Rostami

Target audience: Children aged 3-9 years
Moral lesson: Letting go is not the end of something. It is the beginning of something new.
Visual style: High-quality 3D animated children's illustration (cinematic children's animation), warm golden autumn light, painterly rendering with glowing sunbeams, soft bokeh, rich russet and amber palette.
Format: Portrait orientation, 9:16 aspect ratio, 768x1376 px, RGB, one image per page, 20 pages total.

CHARACTER LOCK (repeat in every prompt):
Rustle - a bright maple leaf with a soft friendly face painted into his surface, two gentle dark eyes, a small kind mouth, and rosy warm cheeks. He has a slim russet-brown stem, gently lobed maple points and a slightly curled edge. His colour changes through the book, exactly as listed on each page, and he is always the SAME leaf shape and face.

SUPPORTING CHARACTERS:
- Amber: a warm golden-orange maple leaf with a cheerful face and a long stem.
- Golden: a bright yellow maple leaf with a laughing face.
- Crimson: a deep red maple leaf with a bold friendly face.
- The blue jay: a vivid blue jay with a white chest, a black crest and bright curious eyes.
- Emma: a little girl about six years old with wavy chestnut hair, a mustard-yellow coat, a rust-red scarf, dark green boots.
- The wind: never drawn as a body or a creature. Show it only as swirling ribbons of air, drifting leaves and soft spiral lines of light.

SETTING: a great maple tree in a park across the autumn, then the fallen-leaf carpet on the ground, then a moonlit night, then dawn.

TEXT RULE: all text is painted into the illustration. Narrative prose carries no quotation marks; spoken lines keep double quotation marks exactly. Avoid contractions that invite stray apostrophes (write "I am", "do not", "it is").

== PAGE 1 (FRONT COVER) ==
Title (embedded in artwork): THE LAST LEAF OF AUTUMN
Credit (embedded in artwork): Created by Hamidreza Rostami
Art: Rustle, a vivid red-and-gold maple leaf with a gentle face, clings to his branch high in a great maple tree, seen close and beautiful, glowing with backlit golden autumn sun. Behind and below him the tree is nearly bare, and the ground is a soft carpet of fallen gold and red leaves. Warm russet and amber palette, glowing bokeh.

== PAGE 2 ==
Text: Rustle loved his branch. It was the perfect branch. High up in the great maple tree.
Art: A wide, beautiful view of the great maple tree in early autumn, full of green and gold leaves, standing in a sunlit park. High on one branch, small but clear, Rustle hangs happily. Warm morning sun, long shadows, soft mist.

== PAGE 3 ==
Text: Just the right spot for catching the morning sun. Just the right place for feeling the cool breeze. And it was the perfect branch for watching the world.
Art: Rustle on his branch in golden morning light, the sun glowing through his leaf so his veins shine. Below him a peaceful park: paths, benches, a pond, tiny distant people. Soft warm light, gentle breeze shown as a few drifting leaves.

== PAGE 4 ==
Text: Rustle had spent all spring growing. All summer dancing in the wind. And now, autumn had come.
Art: A seasonal story in one frame: the same tree with three moods layered softly, spring green buds at the top, full summer green in the middle, golden autumn at the bottom, the light warm and mellow. Rustle hangs at the centre, glowing, a few leaves drifting past him.

== PAGE 5 ==
Text: The other leaves were changing. Turning gold. Turning red. Turning orange. And one by one, they were letting go.
Art: A rich autumn canopy. Around Rustle, leaves are turning gold, red and orange, and several are detached and falling, caught mid-drift on beams of golden light. Rustle is still green among them, clinging to his branch, watching. Warm luminous bokeh.

== PAGE 6 ==
Text: "Time to go, Rustle!" called his friend Amber, as she twirled down to the ground. "The ground is soft!" said Golden, landing beside her. "And the wind is fun!"
Art: Two autumn leaves, Amber (golden-orange) and Golden (bright yellow), both with happy faces, twirling down through the air in a graceful spiral. Amber calls up over her shoulder toward Rustle on his branch above. Golden is just touching the soft leaf-covered ground with a laugh. Warm light, drifting leaves.

== PAGE 7 ==
Text: But Rustle shook his stem. "I am not ready. Not yet."
Art: A close, tender shot of Rustle on his branch, still green, gripping the twig firmly with his stem, his little face set and determined, eyebrows drawn. Behind him the tree is half bare and gold light pours through the gaps. Determined and a little nervous.

== PAGE 8 ==
Text: The days grew cooler. The winds grew stronger. More leaves fell. The tree was getting bare.
Art: A colder, grander scene. The great tree is now mostly bare, its dark branches crossing the pale sky, only a scattering of leaves left. Wind streams through as long sweeping ribbons of air. Several leaves tumble away into the distance. Cool blue-grey light with a low warm sun behind.

== PAGE 9 ==
Text: "You will be the last one!" called Crimson, as she fluttered down. "That is okay," Rustle said. "I like it here."
Art: Crimson, a deep red maple leaf with a bold friendly face, waves up at Rustle as she flutters down through the air. Rustle clings to his branch above, still mostly green with the first gold creeping in at his edges, smiling bravely down at her. Bare branches, cool autumn light.

== PAGE 10 ==
Text: Then, one morning, Rustle noticed something. He was changing too. His edges were turning. From green to gold. From gold to red.
Art: A close-up of Rustle hanging on his twig, looking down at himself in surprise, his eyes wide. His leaf surface is mid-transformation: green in the centre, gold spreading outward, a deep red creeping in along the edges and points, the veins glowing. Bright crisp morning light, dew drops.

== PAGE 11 ==
Text: He was becoming beautiful.
Art: A gorgeous celebration close-up of Rustle at full autumn glory: deep red, gold and orange glowing through him like stained glass, backlit by low golden sun, every vein lit, his face full of wondering joy. Warm bokeh, a few drifting leaves.

== PAGE 12 ==
Text: "You are the most beautiful leaf on the tree!" said a blue jay, landing on a nearby branch. Rustle blushed, if a leaf can blush. "Do you really think so?"
Art: A vivid blue jay with a white chest and black crest perched on a nearby twig, leaning toward Rustle with a bright admiring face, one wing lifted. Rustle glows bright red and gold with warm bashful rosy cheeks, his eyes shy and pleased. Blue autumn sky behind, warm sun.

== PAGE 13 ==
Text: "Of course!" said the blue jay. "Look at you! All those colours!"
Art: The blue jay hops closer on the branch, chatting cheerfully to Rustle, who is puffing up proudly, still blushing. Behind them the tree is bare and the low sun flares between the branches. Warm, friendly, funny.

== PAGE 14 ==
Text: But then the blue jay flew away, and Rustle was alone again.
Art: A quiet, wistful image. The blue jay is a small blue shape flying away into the distance. Rustle hangs alone on the great bare branch, very small against the wide cool sky, his colours still bright but his face lonely. Wide empty space around him, soft grey-gold light.

== PAGE 15 ==
Text: The wind blew. Stronger this time. Rustle held on tight. "I am not ready," he whispered.
Art: A dramatic wind scene. Long swirling ribbons of wind tear past the bare branches, leaves streaking sideways. Rustle clings desperately to his twig, his stem stretched and strained, his leaf bent back, his face frightened but determined, eyes squeezed. Cool dramatic light, motion in everything.

== PAGE 16 ==
Text: That night, Rustle heard a soft sound. It was the wind, singing through the branches.
Art: Night. A huge full moon behind the bare black branches, silver light everywhere. The wind is drawn only as soft luminous spiral ribbons curling through the branches, almost like music. Rustle hangs on his twig, tiny and pale in the moonlight, listening. Deep blues and silver, magical.

== PAGE 17 ==
Text: "Let go, little one," the wind whispered. "It is time." "But I do not want to fall," Rustle said. "Falling is not the end," the wind said. "It is the beginning of something new."
Art: The same moonlit night, closer on Rustle. Soft silver wind ribbons wrap gently around him like a kind hand, curling and lifting his edge. Rustle's face is thoughtful and sad and hopeful all at once, his eyes on the wind, his stem still gripping. His red and gold colours catch a faint silver glow. Tender and hushed.

== PAGE 18 ==
Text: Rustle thought about what the wind had said. He looked down at the ground. It was covered in leaves, a soft colourful carpet. He looked up at the sky. It was clear and full of stars. And he looked at himself. He was the most beautiful he had ever been.
Art: A beautiful three-part view held in one image: below, the ground far down covered in a soft carpet of gold, red and orange leaves; above, a deep clear night sky full of stars; and in the middle Rustle himself, glowing deep red and gold, gazing around him with wonder. Soft moonlight, stars reflected in the colours.

== PAGE 19 ==
Text: The next morning, the sun rose. The wind blew gently. And Rustle made a decision. He took a deep breath, if a leaf can breathe. He loosened his grip. And he let go.
Art: Dawn. Warm pink and gold sunrise light floods the park, the bare tree glowing. Rustle's stem is just releasing from the twig, a tiny gap opening, his whole leaf lifting slightly in a gentle breeze. His face is calm and brave and peaceful. Awe and quiet courage.

== PAGE 20 (BACK COVER) ==
Blurb (embedded in artwork):
Rustle the maple leaf loves his branch. It is the perfect branch, high in the great maple tree, with the best view in the whole park.
When autumn comes and every other leaf lets go, Rustle holds on. He is not ready. Not yet.
A warm story about courage, change, and discovering that letting go can be the beginning of something beautiful.
